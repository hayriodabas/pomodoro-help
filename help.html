Pomodoro help

Pomodoro is a study timer that counts down to an exam. Give a subject its exam day and the hours it needs, and every day the app shows how many rounds are due today. This page explains how it works on iPhone and iPad.

Plan an exam

On the timer screen, today's sheet asks Studying for an exam? Tap Plan an exam. The same item is at the bottom of the subject menu above the dial.

Pick a subject or type a new one, choose the Exam day (tomorrow at the earliest) and set Study before the exam, from 1 to 300 hours. Under Each day the planner shows what that asks of one day, in minutes and in rounds of your focus length.

Tap Save. Today's sheet now lists the subject with an outlined box for every round still to do today. Each finished round fills a box.

How the days share the hours

Today's share is worked out when the day starts and stays fixed until midnight: studying fills it, it does not shrink as you go.

A day that falls short is not lost. Its missing time is spread over the days left before the exam. A day studied beyond its share works the other way: the days after it ask for less.

Study counts from the day the plan starts. Changing only the hours keeps what you have already studied; a new exam day starts the count again from today.

On the exam day the plan is over and the subject leaves today's plan. Its minutes stay in Statistics.

A subject with no exam can have a weekly plan instead: Settings › Subjects, open the subject and turn on Plan the week. What the week still lacks is shared over the days left in the week.

Up next names the planned subject with the most time left today. Choose puts it on the dial.

The dial and the rounds

Drag the handle at the edge of the red wedge round the dial to set the focus length: 5 to 60 minutes, in steps of five. Settings › Lengths allows up to 120 minutes and sets the breaks and how many rounds come before a long break. Two presets are there: 25 · 5 · 15 and 50 · 10 · 20.

Start runs a focus round. A short break follows, and a long break after every fourth round unless you change it. Start breaks automatically in Settings can be turned off.

The check mark beside Pause ends a round early; a round of a minute or more is kept. The circular arrow resets the round.

For open-ended study, choose Stopwatch at the top of the screen. It counts up until you finish.

Statistics

The button at the top right opens Statistics: this week by subject, one square for every 25 minutes, and the list of sessions. On an iPad the week also lies beside the dial.

Add a session by hand records study done without the timer. Tap a session to change its subject, start time or length, or to remove it; Undo brings a removed session back.

Today's sheet shows today's total and your day streak, the number of days in a row with study.

On the Lock Screen

A running round shows on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island on iPhones that have one. Its key pauses the round or goes on with it. Settings › Timer on the Lock Screen turns this off.

The first time a timer starts, iOS asks whether Pomodoro may send notifications. They are the bell at the end of a round and at the end of the break after it. If you say no, the bell rings only while the app is open.

Questions

Does Pomodoro block other apps?

No. During a focus round it counts how often you leave the app, and each time becomes a short stroke over that round's box. It blocks, locks and hides nothing, and it reads no usage data.

Does the timer keep going when I close the app?

Yes. The time is worked out from when the round started, so it is right when you come back.

The bell did not ring

The bell is a notification. If notifications are off for Pomodoro, the timer screen says so and offers Turn on alerts in Settings. The sound follows the silent switch; Settings › Bell at the end turns the sound off.

What is free, and what does Pro add?

Free: the timer and the stopwatch, every length, the bell, the Lock Screen timer, three subjects with their exam plans and weekly plans, today's sheet, this week and last week, and writing, correcting and removing sessions. Pomodoro Pro is a single in-app purchase, not a subscription. It adds unlimited subjects, a daily goal, every earlier week, 30 days and 12 months, time by subject and a spreadsheet (CSV) of every session.

I bought Pro, but it is locked on this device

Open Settings (top left on the timer screen) and tap Restore Purchase, signed in with the Apple Account you bought it with. The Pomodoro Pro sheet has the same button.

Can I get a refund?

Refunds are handled by Apple at reportaproblem.apple.com.

How do I change the language?

Settings › Language opens the iOS settings for Pomodoro, where the app's language can be chosen.

How do I delete everything?

Settings › Delete all data removes every session and subject from this device. Deleting the app does the same.
